Censorship and Conformity in Ray Bradbury's Fahrenheit 451

Censorship

Fahrenheit 451 reveals that the government didn't ban books by force, as is often the case in dystopias. Instead, various social groups condemned certain books because they were offensive or didn’t conform to their worldviews. The government in this book wanted to prevent offending any "minority" imaginable.

On page 54, during dialogue between Captain Beatty and Montag, Beatty said, Don't step on the toes of the dog-lovers, the cat-lovers, doctors, lawyers, merchants, chiefs, Mormons, Baptists, Unitarians, second-generation Chinese, Swedes, Italians, Germans, Texans, Brooklynites, Irishmen, people from Oregon or Mexico. This quote explains why the astounding majority of books have been banned instead of a select few. Many have heard of the saying, "You will always offend someone, no matter what you do." The same concept applies to books. All books will contain content that will upset at least one group of people. However, because the government is set on the goal of keeping all its citizens content, it would better fit their goal to ban all potentially uncomfortable content than allow the content that will upset some.

Because "upsetting" books were shunned and destroyed, saccharine books were left over.

In fact, it was not just books that have been censored. Books are only one type of media that holds ideas that may be uncomfortable or upsetting to certain people.
